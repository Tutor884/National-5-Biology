## 📖 Core drill (8 min)

### The command words

| Word | What SQA wants | Marks lost if you… |
|---|---|---|
| **State / Name / Give** | One short fact. No explanation needed. | …write a paragraph and run out of time |
| **Describe** | Say **what happens** — the sequence of events | …explain why instead |
| **Explain** | Say **why** it happens — the reason/mechanism | …only describe |
| **Suggest** | A sensible idea using your biology — there may be several right answers | …leave it blank because you're not sure |
| **Calculate** | A number, **with the correct unit** | …forget the unit |
| **Predict** | Extend the pattern in the data | …invent something unrelated to the trend |

> 🎯 If a 2-mark question says **explain**, you need **two linked reasons**, not one reason written twice.

### The five calculations that come up

| Type | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Percentage** | part ÷ whole × 100 | 2 000 of 20 000 kJ → 10 % |
| **Percentage change** | (change ÷ **original**) × 100 | 40 → 50 = 10 ÷ 40 × 100 = 25 % increase |
| **Ratio** | Divide both sides by the smaller, **simplify**, keep the **order asked for** | 15 : 45 → **1 : 3** |
| **Average (mean)** | Total ÷ number of values | |
| **Rate** | Quantity ÷ time (**give the unit**, e.g. cm³/min) | 30 cm³ in 5 min = 6 cm³/min |

⚠️ **Ratio traps:** always simplify, and always answer in the **order the question asks**. "Ratio of A to B" ≠ "ratio of B to A".
⚠️ **Percentage change traps:** divide by the **original** value, not the new one.

### Graphs — how to not lose the drawing marks

1. Use the **axes given**, with the **correct scale** — and use **at least half the grid**
2. **Label both axes with units**
3. **Plot points accurately**, join with **straight ruled lines** point to point (unless told to draw a curve of best fit)
4. Add a **key** if there are two lines

### Experimental design — the standard answers

| Question | Standard answer |
|---|---|
| "How would you improve **reliability**?" | **Repeat the experiment and calculate an average** |
| "Why was a **control** used?" | To show the result was **due to the factor being investigated** and nothing else |
| "Give **two variables** to be kept constant" | Anything except the one being changed — volume, concentration, temperature, time, mass, pH |
| "Why is this conclusion **not valid**?" | Only **one** factor was measured / other variables were not controlled / only one trial was carried out |
| "How would you get a **more accurate** value for the optimum?" | Test **more values in narrower intervals** around the peak |

## ✍️ Exam reps (9 min)

1. In an ecosystem survey, 24 of 60 quadrats contained clover. Calculate the percentage. (1)
2. A plant was 12 cm tall and grew to 21 cm in 6 days. Calculate the **average growth rate** per day, with units. (1)
3. A population fell from 850 to 663. Calculate the percentage decrease. (1)
4. In a sample there were 18 producers and 6 primary consumers. Give the **simplest whole number ratio of primary consumers to producers**. (1)
5. A pupil tested one leaf disc at each of four light intensities and concluded that light intensity affects photosynthesis. Give **two** criticisms of the investigation. (2)
6. A pupil found enzyme activity peaked between 35 °C and 45 °C. Describe how to find the optimum temperature **more accurately**. (1)

<details>
<summary>▶ Show answers</summary>

1. 24 ÷ 60 × 100 = **40 %**. **(1)**
2. (21 − 12) ÷ 6 = 9 ÷ 6 = **1.5 cm/day**. **(1)** *(No unit = no mark.)*
3. (850 − 663) ÷ 850 × 100 = 187 ÷ 850 × 100 = **22 %**. **(1)**
4. Consumers : producers = 6 : 18 = **1 : 3**. **(1)** *(Note the order asked for — consumers first.)*
5. Any two: only **one disc** was tested at each intensity, so results are **not reliable** — should repeat and average (1); other variables such as **temperature and CO₂ concentration** may not have been kept constant (1); only **four** intensities were tested. **(2)**
6. Repeat the experiment testing **more temperatures between 35 °C and 45 °C**, at **smaller intervals**. **(1)**
