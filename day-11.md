## 📖 Core drill (6 min)

### DNA structure
- DNA is a **double-stranded helix** held together by **complementary base pairs**
- DNA carries the **genetic information for making proteins**
- Four bases: **adenine (A), cytosine (C), guanine (G), thymine (T)** — they make up the **genetic code**
- **A always pairs with T**; **C always pairs with G**
- The **base sequence determines the amino acid sequence** in a protein
- A **gene** is a **section of DNA that codes for a protein**

> 🧠 **Hook:** **A–T** (Apple Tree) and **C–G** (Car Garage).

### mRNA
**Messenger RNA (mRNA)** is a molecule which:
- carries a **complementary copy** of the genetic code
- **from the DNA, in the nucleus**
- **to a ribosome**, where the **protein is assembled from amino acids**

> ⚠️ Not required at N5: **uracil**, and the detailed stages of transcription and translation. Don't write them — they can't earn marks and they waste time.

### The chain to memorise
**DNA (nucleus) → base sequence → mRNA → ribosome → amino acids assembled → protein**

### Numeracy
If a section of DNA has 200 bases in total and 60 are adenine:
- adenine = 60, so **thymine = 60** (A pairs with T)
- 200 − 120 = 80 remaining, split equally: **cytosine = 40, guanine = 40**

## 🏆 Boss question (3 min)

A mutation changes one base in a gene, altering the amino acid sequence of the enzyme it codes for.

**Explain why this could stop the enzyme working. (3)**

<details>
<summary>▶ Show boss answer</summary>

- The **base sequence determines the amino acid sequence** of the protein (1)
- The **sequence of amino acids determines the protein's shape** (1)
- If the shape of the **active site** changes, it is **no longer complementary to the substrate**, so the enzyme cannot bind it and the reaction does not take place (1) **(3)**
