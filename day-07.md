## 📖 Core drill (6 min)

### Mutation
- A mutation is a **random change to genetic material**
- Mutations may be **neutral**, or confer an **advantage** or a **disadvantage** to survival
- Mutations are **spontaneous** and are the **only source of new alleles**
- **Environmental factors** such as **radiation** and **some chemicals** can **increase the rate** of mutation

> ⚠️ Mutations are **not** caused by need. An organism does not mutate *because* it needs to. They happen randomly, and then the environment selects.

### Variation and adaptation
- New alleles produced by mutation can make plants and animals **better adapted to their environment**
- **Variation within a population** makes it possible for a population to **evolve over time in response to changing environmental conditions**
- A population with **no variation** cannot evolve — that is why low biodiversity is dangerous

### Natural selection — the four steps

1. Species produce **more offspring than the environment can sustain**
2. There is **variation** within the population, and there are **selection pressures**
3. The **best adapted individuals survive to reproduce**, passing on the **favourable alleles** that give the **selective advantage**
4. These **alleles increase in frequency** in the population over generations

> 🧠 **Hook:** **O·V·S·F** — Overproduction, Variation, Survival of the best adapted, Frequency of allele increases.

**Rapid examples:** **MRSA** (antibiotic resistance), insect **resistance to GM crop toxins**, and pesticide resistance.

## ✍️ Exam reps (8 min)

1. Define the term **mutation**. (1)
2. State **two** environmental factors that increase the rate of mutation. (1)
3. Explain why mutation is important for evolution. (2)
4. A population of bacteria is treated with an antibiotic. Most die, but a few survive and the population recovers, now resistant. Explain this using natural selection. (4)

<details>
<summary>▶ Show answers</summary>

1. A **random change to genetic material**. **(1)**
2. **Radiation** and **some chemicals**. **(1)**
3. Mutation is the **only source of new alleles** (1), producing the **variation** that natural selection acts on / allows the population to become better adapted to a changing environment (1). **(2)**
4. Four marks:
   - A **random mutation** had already produced an allele giving **resistance** in a few bacteria — there is **variation** in the population (1)
   - The antibiotic is a **selection pressure** (1)
   - The **resistant bacteria survive and reproduce**, passing on the **favourable allele** (1)
   - The **frequency of the resistance allele increases** in the population (1) **(4)**

**Marking note:** never write that the bacteria "became resistant because of the antibiotic" or "adapted themselves". That is Lamarck, and it scores **zero**. The mutation was **already there**; the antibiotic only **selected** it.

## 🏆 Boss question (3 min)

A gardener sprays greenfly on roses with the same pesticide every year. In year 1 it kills 99 % of the greenfly. By year 6 it kills only 40 %.

a) Explain this change. (3)
b) Suggest **one** alternative method of control the gardener could use. (1)

<details>
<summary>▶ Show boss answer</summary>

a)
- Some greenfly carried a **random mutation** giving **resistance** to the pesticide — **variation** existed in the population (1)
- The pesticide is a **selection pressure**: the resistant greenfly **survive and reproduce**, passing on the **favourable allele** (1)
- Over the generations the **frequency of the resistance allele increases**, so a greater proportion of the population survives the spray (1) **(3)**

b) **Biological control** — introduce a natural predator such as ladybirds. **(1)**

*This question is the whole of key area 6c plus 5d in one. If you nailed it, you are in A territory.*

</details>
