## 📖 Core drill (6 min)

### The membrane
The cell membrane consists of **phospholipids and proteins** and is **selectively permeable** (it lets some molecules through but not others).

### Passive transport
**Passive transport occurs down a concentration gradient and does NOT require energy.**
Two examples: **diffusion** and **osmosis**.

- **Diffusion** — the movement of **molecules** **down a concentration gradient**, from a **higher** to a **lower** concentration.
- **Osmosis** — the movement of **water molecules** from a **higher water concentration** to a **lower water concentration**, **through a selectively permeable membrane**.

> ⚠️ **Word-perfect osmosis:** *water molecules* + *high water concentration to low water concentration* + *through a selectively permeable membrane*. All three parts. "High to low concentration" alone loses the mark because it doesn't say **water**.

### Active transport
**Active transport requires energy** for **membrane proteins** to move molecules and ions **against the concentration gradient**.
(The detailed mechanism is **not required** — don't over-explain.)

| | Direction | Energy? |
|---|---|---|
| Diffusion | **Down** the gradient | No |
| Osmosis | **Down** the water gradient | No |
| Active transport | **Against** the gradient | **Yes** |

### What happens to cells

| Solution | Water moves | Animal cell | Plant cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hypotonic** (higher water conc. outside) | **In** | Swells and **bursts** | Becomes **turgid** (cell wall stops bursting) |
| **Isotonic** (same) | No net movement | Normal | Normal / flaccid |
| **Hypertonic** (lower water conc. outside) | **Out** | **Shrinks** | Becomes **plasmolysed** |

**Key comparison:** the **cell wall** prevents the plant cell bursting, which is why a plant cell becomes turgid where an animal cell would burst.

## ✍️ Exam reps (8 min)

1. Define **osmosis**. (2)
2. State **two** differences between diffusion and active transport. (2)
3. Red blood cells are placed in pure water. Describe and explain what happens. (2)
4. A plant cell is placed in a concentrated salt solution. Name the term describing its appearance afterwards and explain why. (2)
5. Explain why a cell that has been treated with a respiratory inhibitor can still carry out diffusion but not active transport. (2)

<details>
<summary>▶ Show answers</summary>

1. The movement of **water molecules** (1) from a region of **higher water concentration to a region of lower water concentration through a selectively permeable membrane** (1). **(2)**
2. Any two, as a pair of contrasts: diffusion moves substances **down** the gradient, active transport moves them **against** it (1); diffusion requires **no energy**, active transport **requires energy** (1); active transport requires **membrane proteins** (1). **(2)**
3. Water moves **into** the cell by **osmosis** (the water concentration is higher outside) (1); the cell **swells and bursts**, because an animal cell has **no cell wall** (1). **(2)**
4. **Plasmolysed** (1). Water moves **out of the cell by osmosis** because the water concentration outside is lower, so the membrane pulls away from the cell wall (1). **(2)**
5. Diffusion is **passive** and **does not require energy**, so it continues (1). Active transport **requires energy from respiration**, which the inhibitor has stopped, so it cannot take place (1). **(2)**

## 🏆 Boss question (3 min)

Potato discs of equal mass were placed in sucrose solutions of different concentration for 30 minutes and reweighed.

| Sucrose conc. (M) | Change in mass (%) |
|---|---|
| 0.0 | +8 |
| 0.2 | +3 |
| 0.4 | 0 |
| 0.6 | −5 |
| 0.8 | −9 |

a) Explain the result at 0.0 M. (2)
b) What does the result at 0.4 M tell you about the potato cells? (1)

<details>
<summary>▶ Show boss answer</summary>

a) The water concentration is **higher outside** the cells than inside (1), so water moves **into the cells by osmosis**, increasing their mass (1). **(2)**
b) At 0.4 M there is **no net movement of water** — the water concentration of the sucrose solution is **equal to** the water concentration inside the potato cells. **(1)**

*A-grade extra: the 0.4 M point is how you work out the internal concentration of a tissue. Expect this experiment to appear.*

</details>
