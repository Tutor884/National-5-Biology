## 📖 Core drill (6 min)

Copy these out **in your own handwriting**. Twelve definitions, and they are free marks.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Species | Group of organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring |
| Biodiversity | The total variety of species in an ecosystem |
| Population | All the organisms of **one species** in a habitat |
| Community | **All** the organisms (all species) living in a habitat |
| Habitat | The place where an organism lives |
| Ecosystem | The community **plus** the non-living components it interacts with |
| Producer | Organism that makes its own food by photosynthesis (green plant / algae) |
| Consumer | Organism that eats other organisms |
| Herbivore / Carnivore / Omnivore | Eats plants / eats animals / eats both |
| Predator | Animal that hunts and kills another animal |
| Prey | The animal that is hunted |
| Food chain / web | A single feeding pathway / interlinked food chains |

**Niche** — the *role* an organism plays in a community. It covers:
- the resources it needs (light, nutrients…)
- its interactions with others (competition, predation)
- the conditions it can tolerate (e.g. temperature)

**Competition** — happens when **resources are in short supply**.

| | Who competes | What for | Intensity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inter**specific | **Different** species | One or a few shared resources | Less intense |
| **Intra**specific | **Same** species | **All** the resources they need | **More intense** |

> 🧠 **Memory hook:** *Intra* = *internal* = same species = **all** resources = **intense**.

## ✍️ Exam reps (8 min) — write these out

1. State the difference between a **population** and a **community**. (1)
2. Explain why **intraspecific** competition is more intense than interspecific competition. (2)
3. In a food web, all the ladybirds are removed. Ladybirds eat aphids; aphids feed on rose plants; blue tits eat ladybirds.
   a) Describe the effect on the aphid population and explain why. (2)
   b) Describe the effect on the blue tit population. (1)
4. Define the term **niche**. (1)

<details>
<summary>▶ Show answers</summary>

1. A population is all the members of **one species** in a habitat; a community is **all the species / all the organisms** in that habitat. **(1)**
2. Members of the same species need **exactly the same resources** (1), so they compete for **all** of them, whereas different species only overlap on **one or a few** resources (1). **(2)**
3. a) Aphid numbers **increase** (1) because a predator has been removed / fewer aphids are eaten (1). **(2)**
   b) Blue tit numbers **decrease** — they have lost a food source. **(1)**
   *(If you wrote "blue tits might eat something else instead", that's good biology but SQA wants the direct effect first.)*
4. The **role** an organism plays in its community — the resources it uses, its interactions with other organisms, and the conditions it tolerates. **(1)**

**Marking note:** "where it lives" for niche = **0 marks**. That's a habitat. This is the single most common lost mark in this key area.

</details>

## 🏆 Boss question (3 min)

Two species of barnacle live on the same rocky shore. Species A is found only on the upper shore. In the lab, species A grows perfectly well in lower-shore conditions when species B is absent.

**Explain what this tells you about the distribution of species A on the shore. (2)**

<details>
<summary>▶ Show boss answer</summary>

Species A is **not** restricted by abiotic conditions — it can tolerate the lower shore (1). It is excluded from the lower shore by **interspecific competition** with species B, which is the better competitor there (1). **(2)**

*This is the classic Scottish barnacle example the SQA course notes point at. If you got both marks, take the +5 XP.*

</details>
