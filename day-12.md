## 📖 Core drill (6 min)

### Proteins
- The **variety of protein shapes and functions arises from the sequence of amino acids**
- Functions: **structural**, **enzymes**, **hormones**, **antibodies**, **receptors**

> 🧠 **Hook:** **S·E·H·A·R** — Structural, Enzymes, Hormones, Antibodies, Receptors.

### Enzymes
- Enzymes are **biological catalysts**, **made by all living cells**
- They **speed up cellular reactions** and are **unchanged in the process** (so they can be reused)
- The **shape of the active site** is **complementary to its specific substrate(s)**
- An **enzyme–substrate complex** forms, facilitating the reaction
- Enzyme action results in **product(s)**

**Two reaction types:**

| Type | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Degradation** | One large substrate → **broken down** into smaller products | Amylase: starch → maltose · Catalase: hydrogen peroxide → water + oxygen · Pepsin: protein → peptides |
| **Synthesis** | Two or more small substrates → **built up** into one larger product | Phosphorylase: glucose-1-phosphate → starch |

### Optimum conditions and denaturation
- Each enzyme is **most active in its optimum conditions**
- Enzymes, **and other proteins**, can be affected by **temperature** and **pH**
- Enzymes can be **denatured**, resulting in a **change in their shape**, which **affects the rate of reaction**

**Reading an enzyme graph:**
- Rate **rises** to a **peak** = the **optimum**
- Rate **falls** after the peak = the enzyme is being **denatured** — its **active site changes shape** so it is **no longer complementary to the substrate**
- On the **pH** graph, the same logic applies either side of the optimum

> ⚠️ Never write "the enzyme dies" or "the enzyme is killed". Enzymes are molecules. Write **denatured**.

## ✍️ Exam reps (8 min)

1. State **three** functions of proteins other than as enzymes. (3)
2. Explain why an enzyme can only act on one substrate. (2)
3. An enzyme has an optimum temperature of 40 °C. Explain why its activity is very low at 70 °C. (2)
4. Give **one** example of a degradation reaction and **one** of a synthesis reaction, naming the enzyme, substrate and product in each. (2)
5. Why can a small quantity of enzyme catalyse a large quantity of substrate? (1)

<details>
<summary>▶ Show answers</summary>

1. Any three: **structural**, **hormones**, **antibodies**, **receptors**. **(3)**
2. The **active site** of the enzyme has a **specific shape** (1) which is **complementary only to its specific substrate**, so no other substrate can bind (1). **(2)**
3. At 70 °C the enzyme is **denatured** (1); the **shape of its active site changes**, so it is **no longer complementary to the substrate** and the rate of reaction falls (1). **(2)**
4. **Degradation:** amylase breaks down **starch** into **maltose** (1). **Synthesis:** phosphorylase builds **glucose-1-phosphate** into **starch** (1). *(Catalase: hydrogen peroxide → water + oxygen is also accepted for degradation.)* **(2)**
5. The enzyme is **unchanged in the reaction**, so it can be **reused** on further substrate molecules. **(1)**

**Marking note:** for Q3 the SQA mark scheme wants the **shape/active site change**, not just the word "denatured". Always give both halves.

</details>

## 🏆 Boss question (4 min)

A pupil investigated catalase activity by measuring the height of oxygen foam produced from hydrogen peroxide at five pH values. Each pH was tested once.

| pH | Foam height (mm) |
|---|---|
| 3 | 2 |
| 5 | 14 |
| 7 | 31 |
| 9 | 12 |
| 11 | 1 |

a) State the optimum pH of catalase from these results. (1)
b) Explain the result at pH 11. (2)
c) Give **one** way the reliability of the results could be improved. (1)

<details>
<summary>▶ Show boss answer</summary>

a) **pH 7**. **(1)**
b) At pH 11 the enzyme is **denatured** (1) — the **shape of its active site has changed** so it is **no longer complementary to hydrogen peroxide**, so very little oxygen is produced (1). **(2)**
c) **Repeat the experiment at each pH and calculate an average.** **(1)**
   *(Not "test more pH values" — that improves the **validity/accuracy of the optimum**, not reliability. If the question asks how to find a **more accurate optimum**, then you would say "test more pH values between 5 and 9".)*

</details>

> 🎯 That distinction — **reliability = repeat and average**, **accuracy of an optimum = narrower intervals** — is worth a mark in most N5 papers. Learn both.
