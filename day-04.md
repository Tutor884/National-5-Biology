## 📖 Core drill (6 min)

### Energy loss along a food chain

When energy is transferred from one level to the next, **the majority is lost** as:
- **heat**
- **movement**
- **undigested materials**

Only a **very small quantity** is used for **growth**, and only that is available to the next level.

> 🧠 **Hook:** **H·M·U** — Heat, Movement, Undigested. Three losses, three marks available.

**Consequence:** food chains are short — there isn't enough energy left to support many levels.

### Pyramids

| | Pyramid of numbers | Pyramid of energy |
|---|---|---|
| Shows | The **number of organisms** at each level | The **energy** available at each level |
| Units | (count) | **kJ/m²/year** |
| Shape | Can be **irregular** | **Always a true pyramid** |

**Why numbers pyramids go irregular:**
- A **large producer** — e.g. **one oak tree** feeding thousands of insects → bottom bar is tiny
- **Parasites** — e.g. hundreds of fleas on one fox → top bar is huge

**Why energy pyramids are always true pyramids:** because energy is **always lost** between levels, so each level **always** has less energy than the one below it — regardless of the size or number of organisms.

## ✍️ Exam reps (8 min)

1. State **three** ways energy is lost between levels of a food chain. (3)
2. Give **one** difference between a pyramid of numbers and a pyramid of energy. (1)
3. Explain why a pyramid of numbers for the food chain
   **oak tree → caterpillars → blue tits → sparrowhawks** is **not** a true pyramid shape. (2)
4. Explain why a pyramid of energy is **always** a true pyramid shape. (2)
5. A producer level contains 20 000 kJ/m²/year. The primary consumers contain 2 000 kJ/m²/year. Calculate the percentage of energy transferred. (1)

<details>
<summary>▶ Show answers</summary>

1. **Heat**, **movement**, **undigested materials**. **(3)**
2. Any one: numbers shows **count of organisms**, energy shows **energy available**; energy is measured in **kJ/m²/year**; numbers can be **irregular**, energy is **always a true pyramid**. **(1)**
3. The oak tree is a **single, very large producer** (1), so the producer bar is **narrower** than the caterpillar bar above it, even though it supports them (1). **(2)**
4. Energy is **always lost** (as heat, movement and undigested material) at each transfer (1), so each successive level **always has less energy** than the one below (1). **(2)**
5. 2 000 ÷ 20 000 × 100 = **10 %**. **(1)**

**Marking note:** Q3 — "because the tree is big" on its own is weak. Say it is **one large producer supporting many consumers**, so the bar is **narrower**.

</details>

## 🏆 Boss question (3 min)

A farmer is told that a given area of land can feed more people if it grows wheat for humans than if it grows wheat to feed cattle that humans then eat.

**Use your knowledge of energy transfer to explain why. (3)**

<details>
<summary>▶ Show boss answer</summary>

- Eating wheat directly makes humans **primary consumers**; eating beef makes them **secondary consumers**, adding an extra level to the food chain (1).
- At **each** transfer the **majority of energy is lost** as heat, movement and undigested materials (1).
- So less energy is available at the higher level, and the same land therefore supports **fewer people** when cattle are involved (1). **(3)**

</details>
