## 📖 Core drill (6 min)

**Biotic** = living factors: competition, disease, food availability, grazing, predation.
**Abiotic** = non-living factors: light intensity, moisture, pH, temperature.

> 🧠 **Hook:** bi-**O**-tic = **O**rganisms. A-biotic = **A**bsent of life.

### Measuring abiotic factors

| Factor | Instrument |
|---|---|
| Light intensity | Light meter |
| Soil moisture | Moisture meter |
| pH | pH meter / probe |
| Temperature | Thermometer |

**Sources of error** and how to fix them — learn this pattern, it is worth 2 marks over and over:

| Error | How to minimise it |
|---|---|
| Shading the light meter with your body | Stand out of the way / hold meter away from you |
| Meter probe not cleaned between readings | Wipe/clean the probe each time |
| Meter not left long enough to settle | Wait for the reading to stabilise |
| One reading only | Take **several readings and average** them |

### Sampling

| Method | Used for | Limitations / errors |
|---|---|---|
| **Quadrat** | Plants and slow-moving animals | Too few quadrats; placed non-randomly (biased); miscounting plants at the edge |
| **Pitfall trap** | Small ground-dwelling invertebrates | Predators eat trapped prey inside; rain fills it; trap not covered so birds eat catch; too few traps |

**How to minimise:** use **more** quadrats/traps, place them **randomly**, use the **same size** quadrat, empty traps regularly, put a **raised cover** over the trap.

### Paired-statement keys
Two opposite statements at each step; follow the one that matches, which leads to the next pair or a name. Be able to **use** one and **construct** one.

### Indicator species
Species whose **presence or absence** indicates the level of pollution / environmental quality.
Example: lichens — abundant in **clean** air, absent where SO₂ pollution is high. Mayfly nymphs indicate **clean** water; bloodworms/rat-tailed maggots indicate **polluted** water.

## ✍️ Exam reps (8 min)

1. Name **two** abiotic factors and **two** biotic factors. (2)
2. A pupil used a light meter under a tree canopy. Give **one** source of error and how to minimise it. (2)
3. A pupil used five quadrats to estimate daisy numbers in a football pitch. Give **two** ways to improve the reliability of the results. (2)
4. Explain what is meant by an **indicator species**. (1)
5. A pitfall trap contained one beetle and several beetle legs. Suggest why. (1)

<details>
<summary>▶ Show answers</summary>

1. Abiotic — any two of: light intensity, moisture, pH, temperature. Biotic — any two of: competition, disease, food availability, grazing, predation. **(2)**
2. Error: the pupil's **shadow/body blocks light** from the meter (1). Minimise: **stand away from / do not shade the meter** when taking the reading (1). **(2)**
   *(Also acceptable: meter not allowed to settle → wait for a stable reading.)*
3. Any two: use **more quadrats** (1); place them **randomly** (1); use the **same size** of quadrat throughout (1); repeat and **average** (1). **(2)**
4. A species whose **presence or absence** indicates the **environmental quality / level of pollution**. **(1)**
5. A **predator** was also caught in the trap and ate the other beetles. **(1)**

**Marking note:** for Q3, "be more careful" or "do it again" scores **nothing**. Say *more*, *random*, or *average*.

</details>

## 🏆 Boss question (3 min)

A student measured soil pH at 10 points across a field and found daisies only where pH was above 6.5. The student concluded that **pH determines daisy distribution**.

**Give one reason why this conclusion may not be valid, and suggest how the investigation could be improved. (2)**

<details>
<summary>▶ Show boss answer</summary>

**Not valid because:** only **one** abiotic factor was measured — other factors (light intensity, soil moisture, temperature) or biotic factors (grazing, competition) could be responsible (1).
**Improvement:** measure the **other abiotic factors** at the same points / repeat the investigation in another field to confirm the pattern (1). **(2)**

*"Correlation is not cause" is an A-grade instinct. Every time a question gives you one factor and one pattern, ask what else was changing.*

</details>
